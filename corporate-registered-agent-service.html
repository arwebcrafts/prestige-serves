When the registered agent isn't where the state says it is

Serving a business entity should be simple: locate the registered agent, deliver the documents, file the proof. In practice, Secretary of State filings lag, agents relocate, and receptionists are trained to say "not here" to anyone carrying a clipboard.

We verify the entity's current agent for service before we ever knock, cross-checking Secretary of State records against our own skip-tracing data. Where the filing is outdated, we locate the business's actual operating address, an officer authorized to accept, or the correct corporate office — so a lapsed filing doesn't become the reason your case sits still.

Every attempt is made with the same discretion we'd use on a private residence: no branded vehicles, no announcing "process service" at the front desk. The goal is a completed, defensible serve — not a warning shot that sends a defendant's counsel scrambling before you've finished the job.

Waiting on the SOS?

If the Secretary of State's office is backlogged on confirming an agent for service, we can independently verify and attempt the serve in the meantime — often faster than waiting on a state response, and with documentation that supports a due diligence or substitute service declaration if the court requires one.

Frequently asked questions

What if the registered agent on file with the Secretary of State is outdated?

We cross-reference the filing against current operating and business addresses using our skip-tracing resources, and attempt service at the location most likely to result in valid completion. If the listed agent has genuinely moved with no forwarding record, we'll advise you before additional attempts are billed.

Can you serve an officer directly instead of the registered agent?

Yes. California law allows service on a corporation's registered agent, or on certain officers and authorized persons. If you already know an authorized individual, let us know and we'll attempt service on them directly.

Do you provide due diligence documentation if the case requires substitute service?

Yes. Each attempt is logged with the date, time, address, and what our server observed, with geotagged and timestamped photos — the same standard we use for individual due diligence declarations — so the record supports a substitute service filing if personal service isn't completed.

How is this priced differently from your individual serve tiers?

Corporate service requires agent verification and often more discretion to avoid alerting company staff or counsel before service is complete, which is additional work beyond a standard residential or workplace attempt. The $145 base reflects that, with the same Rush, Priority, and Emergency turnaround options available at a proportional rate.
