Frequently Asked Questions

How long do I have to serve the defendant after filing?

Under California Rule of Court 3.110, the complaint generally must be served within 60 days after it is filed, and courts can impose sanctions or set a hearing when service is late. Separate statutes of limitations govern when the case must be filed. Your attorney will know which deadlines apply, and we work backward from them.

What if the defendant is avoiding service?

We start with a skip trace to confirm the address, then attempt service at different times of day. If the defendant is at a known home or workplace but not answering, a stakeout can wait for the right window. Every attempt is logged, so you have a record of diligence if a court asks for one.

Can you serve an uninsured driver or a driver who left the scene?

Yes, once you have a name. These defendants often have outdated addresses, so we usually start with a skip trace. We cannot identify an unknown driver; identification comes from the police report, DMV records, or discovery.

Do you watch plaintiffs or gather evidence on their activity?

No. Our stakeouts exist only to complete service on a defendant or witness. Investigation and evidence-gathering surveillance require a licensed private investigator, and we do not offer that work.
